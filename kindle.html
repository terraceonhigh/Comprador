Your Kindle won't show up on your Mac?

If you bought a Kindle in the last year and it stopped appearing when you plug it in, you didn't break anything. Amazon changed how the newer models talk to a computer, and the change doesn't sit well with macOS.

What changed

The 2024 Kindles, including the basic Kindle, the 12th-generation Paperwhite, the Colorsoft, and the Scribe, switched to MTP. Older Kindles showed up as a plain USB drive you could open in Finder. The new ones don't. Windows reads MTP without any help, but macOS doesn't, so a 2024 Kindle charges happily and never appears in the sidebar. Amazon worked around this by tucking a USB file manager into its Send to Kindle app.

Mount it in Finder instead

Comprador shows the Kindle in Finder like a USB drive, so you can drag books straight onto it. There's nothing to set up on the Kindle, and nothing to pay. It's free and open source.

  1. Plug the Kindle in with a cable that carries data, not a charge-only one.
  2. It appears in Finder's sidebar under Locations, named as your Kindle.
  3. Open it and drop your books into the documents folder. Eject it before you unplug.

Questions

Which Kindles need this?

The 2024 models: the basic Kindle, the 12th-generation Paperwhite, the Colorsoft, and the Scribe. Pre-2024 Kindles still mount as a normal USB drive.

Why doesn't my Kindle show up on my Mac?

The new Kindles speak MTP, and macOS has no built-in MTP support. The Kindle connects and charges, but Finder never lists it until something on the Mac translates MTP.

Do I need Amazon's Send to Kindle app?

No. Comprador puts the Kindle in Finder so you can drag books onto it. Send to Kindle works as well, but it lives in its own window and only handles Kindles.

Does it work on Apple Silicon?

Yes. It's a native app, signed and notarized by Apple, for macOS 13 or later.

Is it safe?

Free and open source, no telemetry. It talks to the Kindle over USB and serves the files to Finder over a loopback connection the network can't reach. Nothing leaves your Mac.
